Chosen method
-------------
Use stress-free element birth in the current converged/deformed configuration,
activate the full physical constitutive/coupled operator immediately, and let
the ordinary global nonlinear equilibrium solve remove the construction-induced
out-of-balance force. Do not ramp stiffness or constitutive response from a
near-zero value and do not reuse pre-void constitutive history.

Why this method
---------------
It is the direct multiphysics extension of the already-authoritatively-passed
A4L/HM architecture. TRM-T2/T3 already provide fresh constitutive state,
zero birth stress, u_birth-relative mechanical kinematics, and explicit
absolute p_L0/T0 placement states. T4 therefore adds a real nonzero body load
and tests global equilibrium restoration without inventing a new continuation
mechanism.

Established analogues reviewed before T4
----------------------------------------
1. Abaqus Model Change / element activation documentation (2023-2025 docs):
   element addition uses the activation configuration as the reference for
   newly added material; full physical properties are used for full activation.
   https://docs.software.vt.edu/

2. PLAXIS staged-construction method: staged geometry/material changes create
   an out-of-balance force that is resolved by the nonlinear calculation while
   activated soil has its physical stiffness/strength available in the phase.
   Bentley PLAXIS documentation and staged-construction theory.
   https://www.bentley.com/software/plaxis-2d/

3. FLAC3D staged excavation/backfill practice: zones can be assigned the null
   model for excavation and later restored/assigned material for backfill;
   equilibrium is then re-solved with the active zones and loads.
   https://docs.itascacg.com/

4. Qin et al. (2020), Stress-free-state based structural analysis and
   construction control theory for staged construction bridges, Advances in
   Bridge Engineering 1:1. The paper formulates staged construction using
   stress-free-state variables and equilibrium of the current construction
   stage.
   https://doi.org/10.1186/s43251-020-00001-y

5. Richards & Powrie (1994), Finite element analysis of construction sequences
   for propped retaining walls, ICE Geotechnical Engineering 107(4), 207-216.
   Construction sequence is treated as a sequence of equilibrium states rather
   than by artificial stiffness dilution.
   https://doi.org/10.1680/igeng.1994.26962
